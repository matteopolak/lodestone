# Generation animal population

## What it is

Initial animal population proposes creature packs when a column reaches full generation and materialises valid candidates through one shared per-world consumer. Native startup, browser worlds and newly explored columns use the same claim and completion state.

## How it works

**Proposal.** `lodestone_worldgen::spawners::BiomeSpawners` compiles biome attributes into the shared spawn settings: `minecraft:gameplay/natural_mob_spawns` supplies an `overlay` with ordered `spawns_by_category` lists and `spawn_costs`; `minecraft:gameplay/creature_world_gen_spawn_probability` defaults to `0.1` (snowy plains and ice spikes `0.07`, badlands and eroded badlands `0.03`, wooded badlands `0.04`). A fresh probability draw precedes every pack and stops at the first failure (expected `0.1 / (1 - 0.1) = 1/9` packs). Each success draws a creature species by weight, an inclusive pack size and candidate positions within the column; an empty creature list yields none.

Entry counts accept integer constants (equal bounds) and `minecraft:uniform` inclusive ranges with distinct endpoints (one bounded draw). All 811 entries in the 67-biome census fit (605 constants, 206 distinct uniforms); equal-endpoint uniforms are unsupported because they still consume a draw. Malformed attributes, unknown count providers and over-large ranges fail instead of yielding empty lists. This does not establish full natural-spawn or placement parity.

Candidate Y uses the finished column's fused motion-blocking height summaries: ordinary creatures take the first free row above motion-blocking or fluid cells excluding leaves; parrots and ocelots include leaves. Heights are relative to `min_y`; short grass does not raise them, so animals stand in vegetation above the grass block. No extra scan or generation request.

**Claims.** Only `Full` columns carry candidates. `GenerationSpawnBatch` is a clone-shared claim and completion state per nonempty candidate list (copying a column preserves it; empty columns allocate none). Before publishing a claimable batch the resident source retains its exact column through the authoritative terrain-edit boundary, so eviction and a later visit recover the column and completion state instead of a new generation identity. `GenerationPopulation` keeps only unresolved candidates. Generation workers retain population-bearing Full terrain under its coordinate gate, then append the batch to a transient unresolved queue; tick discovery uses a try-lock and bounded result count. Claimed batches stay published until completion so a dropped consumer can return unresolved work; retained columns take precedence over reusable generation outputs, and republishing a reconstructed column reuses its population identity.

`Terrain263ChunkSource` proposes packs itself, Overworld only, the first time a chunk is generated (its `populated` set), using the biome at the chunk's minimum corner at the top of the world and `spawn_candidates_for_chunk` with `worldgen_data::bundled_spawners_by_builtin()`; it publishes straight to its pending queue (so `retain_generation_population` retains nothing). A column regenerated after leaving every cache unedited gets no packs. The Nether's strider packs are not proposed (their standing position needs a ceiling-aware downward scan) and the End generates none.

**Consumption.** The shared world tick owns one `GenerationPopulation`: it admits the source's bounded `pending_generation_spawn_batches` (including resident columns beyond the natural tick area) and snapshots only the sparse coordinates unresolved batches need. Placement runs outside the mob lock; accepted candidates enter `MobSim::spawn_species` under it with the creature category and persistence flag, and the ordinary end-of-tick publication carries them to connections. It never replaces the live simulation. Native startup installs terrain, restores the roster and releases its tick hold before this consumer runs; browser worlds use the same tick body.

Placement classification separates unavailable from rejected: a missing or incomplete column or exhausted light budget retains the candidate; success or a definitive invalid placement removes it once. A batch completes when its last candidate is removed, so persistence may discard the transient handoff; dropping a consumer restores only unresolved candidates, never materialised animals. Generation population bypasses the natural caps and player-distance exclusions but real placement rules apply. Selection uses deterministic chunk seeds without claiming reference RNG ordering or retry parity: each member has one candidate and its wander clamps to the generated column; the one-block land-pathfindability adjustment is not modelled. The validator starts one light cycle per world tick; switching from the sparse generation view to the natural view keeps its four-column admission budget and cached light. Natural spawning can use a partial resident snapshot (a missing neighbour is skipped, refreshed next tick, no generation requested).

## How to change it

- Probability or pack selection: update the biome parser and `spawn_stage::spawn_candidates_for_chunk`; probability must stay below one so the repeat gate terminates. `biome_spawners_current` checks every ordered list, count, cost and probability (deleting attributes or overrides supplies negative controls). Supporting degenerate uniforms means retaining the provider kind through both count consumers, not widening the endpoint check. Scripted draws distinguish admission at probability `0.1`: `0.27` admits zero packs, `0.07, 0.37` one, `0.03, 0.08, 0.42` two.
- A species-specific heightmap: change the surface-height closure in `lodestone_server::chunk::terrain263`'s generation-spawn proposal, reusing the client motion summaries (fluids and leaves tag); the generation motion summary has snow-support provenance and is not this heightmap. The bundled placement test samples biomes at independently probability-positive coordinates and generates at most two creature-bearing land contexts; its Y probes are a prefilter, not a substitute for the full-column grass-support assertions.
- `GenerationPopulation` changes: a false materialisation result must mean no entity was created; keep terrain and light classification outside the mob lock; never replace the simulation.
- Preserve the batch object across every resident column clone (rebuilding from the same candidates creates a second identity and duplicates population); publish only after terrain retention succeeds; keep retention on generation workers; a population claim must not make an unmodified halo look like edited terrain. Synchronous legacy drains are for storage fixtures only. Native chunk records refuse pending batches and omit completed ones; saving chunks and the roster stays separate work and is not crash-atomic.

## Configuration

Biome attributes control probability (default `0.1`, accepted `[0, 0.9999999]`), category weights and pack counts. The parser also accepts the old top-level `spawners`, `spawn_costs` and `creature_spawn_probability` fields with `minCount`/`maxCount` as input compatibility (current attributes take precedence and borrow nothing from them); remove it with its fixtures once the live resolver bundle and synthetic biomes use attributes. `PENDING_BATCH_LIMIT` bounds unresolved batches to 256; `CANDIDATE_BUDGET` limits each consumer cycle to 256 placement decisions; the validator shares the natural spawner's four-column light budget. The `spawn_mobs` game rule gates the path: off, every pending candidate is rejected and drained (not held). Spawned animals take their species' category and count in the natural-spawn census. No environment flags.

## Dependencies

`lodestone-worldgen` (biome settings, deterministic RNG, candidate positions); `lodestone-server::generation_population` (claims, deferred work); server terrain snapshots, the natural spawner's placement rules and light cache, and `MobHandle`. Entity persistence saves materialised animals through the ordinary roster.
