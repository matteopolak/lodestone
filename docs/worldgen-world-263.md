# 26.3 world source

## What it is

`lodestone_server::Terrain263ChunkSource` serves the columns of one dimension generated by `lodestone_worldgen::terrain263::Terrain263`: the 26.3 noise fill, surface rules, carvers and real biomes (multi-noise for the Overworld and Nether, the End's own source), structures, then 26.3 placed-feature decoration (`lodestone_worldgen_feature_26_3`). It serves every generated dimension and world type: Normal, Large Biomes and Amplified Overworlds (`worldgen_data::overworld_chunk_source`, `overworld_chunk_source_of_type`, `overworld_chunk_source_checked`), the single-biome world (`Terrain263::with_fixed_biome` via `single_biome_chunk_source`), and the Nether and End siblings on the integrated and dedicated servers (`nether_chunk_source`, `end_chunk_source`). `lodestone-shell`'s `preset_chunk_source` picks among them.

## How it works

`Terrain263::with_settings(seed, settings)` compiles the bundled tables for one noise settings document: an Overworld preset (-64..320, sky light), `nether` (0..256, no sky light, noise only to 128) or `end` (0..256). A chunk spans the whole build range; above a Nether or End noise range blocks are the fill's (air) and biome cells come from the biome query at their own height. Decoration reads the generator's own depth (128 in the Nether, capping a huge fungus) and the dimension's sky light. Two operations:

- `shaped(cx, cz)` runs fill, surface, carving and biome cells and keeps decorator-space blocks (`ChunkData`) in a bounded cache. World-generation heightmaps are taken after carving and frozen; live ones are recomputed from the blocks a decoration window supplies (`ChunkData::with_states`).
- `decorate_source(source, window)` runs the Decorator over the 3x3 chunks around `source` and returns its writes in order; `window` supplies current blocks of any chunk differing from shaped terrain.

`Terrain263ChunkSource::full_states(cx, cz)` is a target's final column: shaped terrain, then decoration of the nine surrounding chunks in `DECORATION_SOURCE_OFFSETS` order, each reading the blocks earlier ones left in the 5x5 chunks around the target. A column is a pure function of seed and coordinates, but adjacent targets apply sources in their own order, so a feature depending on an earlier neighbour's blocks (a tree whose space another took) can differ across a border.

Two state spaces meet: the decorator's 26.3 `State` (`u16`) and the server's canonical `StateId`. `Terrain263` builds both directions by name and properties at load (`canonical`, `feature_state`); `unmapped_states` must be zero. Biomes are `BiomeId`s named `minecraft:<path>`; the canonical biome enum carries `dappled_forest` after the sorted 26.2 names, leaving 26.2 identities unchanged.

### Structures

`terrain263::structures` (`Terrain263::with_structures`) runs the structure machinery (`StructureRegistry`, jigsaw assembler, templates, processors) over the 26.3 bundled data:

- `structure_starts` finds starts from placement sets (spacing, separation, salt, exclusion zones) and 26.3 surface heights. Strongholds use a concentric-ring layout with the legacy LCG random, first biome match taken without a further draw.
- `structure_refs` collects every start within `REFS_RADIUS` chunks of a column (cache of `REFS_CAPACITY`).
- `beardifier_for` turns nearby pieces into the terrain adaptation `fill_chunk` applies during density evaluation (villages and ancient cities sit on beard, buried structures carve their box).
- `Decorator::decorate_with_structures` asks `place_structures` at the start of each decoration step for the structures generating in it (strongholds and mineshafts at step 4, villages at step 3; `STRUCTURE_RUNTIME_ORDER`), before that step's features, with live heightmaps.

Embedded structure data is 26.3's (`just regen-worldgen-structures`); the parsers accept two 26.3 shape changes (block states as a string or `{id, properties}`; NBT palette entries as `id`/`properties`).

Verified against a real 26.3 server (`scripts/worldgen-oracle-26-3/StructureOracle263.java`, seed 42, `tests/overworld_263_structures.rs`): all 118 starts in chunks -60..=60 and the first three stronghold positions at the right chunk with no extras; 95 of 118 match box, Y and piece count exactly and the rest horizontally; all 128 stronghold ring positions; the village jigsaw's random draws over 23,339 trace lines (`JIGSAW_TRACE=1` with the logging patch under the oracle's `patches/`); base column heights; and a control that a source built without structures serves no village. Known differences: a shipwreck, ocean ruin or swamp hut settles its Y at start here and at piece write there; a monument start has 27 pieces here and one there. Unverified block for block: template placement and processors, the beardifier's terrain effect, mineshaft and portal terrain writes.

Loot chests and fixed-mob spawners come from `structure_loot` (`chests_for_chunk`, `spawners_for_chunk`) over a column's completed starts after decoration: a chest's block is set when the template asks, its entity kept only while the final block is a chest. Every self-named structure container table is bundled (test `every_self_named_loot_table_is_bundled`); other entity blocks placed without data get a default empty entity. A fortress draws chest seeds while placing pieces, so `Terrain263::place_structures` returns them as `CodedLoot`, `decorate_source_full` carries them in `SourceDecoration`, and the source rolls those in the target chunk with `chests_from_coded` (witnesses `a_shipwreck_serves_filled_chests`, `fortress_placement_chests_reach_the_chunk_source`). Only the production constructors attach structures; bare `Terrain263ChunkSource::new` generates none.

### Decoration block entities

A monster room attaches a chest per placed chest (with its drawn loot seed) and a spawner (skeleton, zombie, zombie or spider by the draw); a tree's beehive decorator attaches a nest with one `ticks_in_hive` per bee. `full_states_with_block_entities` returns them as `GeneratedBlockEntity` values, kept only while the final block at the position is its own and one per position, and `generate` adds them. Test: `tests/overworld_263_block_entities.rs` (seed 42, chunk (-24, -33)) with a control that an entity over the wrong block is dropped.

### Verification

`scripts/worldgen-oracle-26-3/WorldOracle263.java` runs the real server for a target as the source does (fill, surface and carve the 5x5 chunks over the real multi-noise biome source, then real feature placement for the nine chunks in production order) and prints one hash per 16-row section plus one for the column. `crates/lodestone-server/tests/overworld_263_oracle.rs` compares `Terrain263ChunkSource::column` with the committed `tests/fixtures/world-oracle-26-3/overworld-42.txt`, names the first differing section, and its control reruns another seed requiring every target to differ. A leading `nether` or `end` switches settings, biome source, range and sky light; `tests/nether_end_263_oracle.rs` checks both (`nether-42.txt`, `end-42.txt`; pillar-free End targets are seed-independent, so at least one must differ). Regenerate (container runtime needed, no host Java):

```
scripts/worldgen-oracle-26-3/run.sh WorldOracle263 42 0 0 7 -3 -9 12 30 30 | grep -v 'WARN\]' \
  > crates/lodestone-server/tests/fixtures/world-oracle-26-3/overworld-42.txt
```

Diagnosing a mismatch: `run.sh WorldOracle263 42 <cx> <cz> dump` prints every non-air block of the target and after each source; `LODESTONE_DUMP=cx,cz cargo test -p lodestone-server --test overworld_263_oracle dump_target -- --ignored --nocapture` prints the served column the same way. Diff the sorted outputs, then narrow to the first differing source and the first placed feature (`trace` mode). One real find: the red-mushroom footprint check uses no tree height, so only the stem column is tested, not cap rows.

### Performance

`cargo run --release -p lodestone-server --example bench_overworld_263 -- 42 <radius> new` times one cold parallel batch (radius 4 is 81 columns, 8 is 289). Compare CPU time from `/usr/bin/time -l`, not wall clock: 81 columns took 3.9 to 4.0 s of user CPU, 289 took 11.4 to 12.1 s with 210 MB peak RSS; quiet single-column latency was p50 22.8 ms, with a cold source's first column the outlier (about 250 ms, serial 5x5 window). One memo per quart cell (`Terrain263::build_shaped`) keeps surface rules from resampling six climate noises per block (shaped chunk about 10 ms). Decoration is about 9 sources per column at about 1.3 ms each, with a sorting (not hashing) change journal (`Level::drain_changes`). It is recomputed per target (only the first source sees pristine terrain, so results cannot be shared without changing semantics); `columns` groups a batch so the union of windows builds once in parallel (`GROUP`, `WARM_LIMIT` in `terrain263.rs`).

## How to change it

- A new decoration feature type goes in `lodestone-worldgen-feature-26-3` ([worldgen features](worldgen-features-26-3.md)); nothing here changes.
- To change what a column's neighbours see, change `full_states`; `WINDOW_RADIUS` is two chunks (nine sources, each reading one beyond itself).
- A world preset: `Terrain263::with_settings(seed, "<noise settings name>")` accepts any Overworld-shaped bundled document (`overworld`, `large_biomes`, `amplified`).
- Generation-time mob packs: `propose_generation_spawns` runs once per Overworld chunk (the `populated` set) at the biome of the chunk's minimum corner at the top of the world and calls `spawn_stage::spawn_candidates_for_chunk` with `worldgen_data::bundled_spawners_by_builtin()`. Parrots and ocelots stand on the motion-blocking heightmap, everything else on no-leaves; the tick loop places the batch. The Nether (striders) and End propose none.
- A feature attaching a block entity calls `Level::attach_block_entity` with a `PlacedBlockEntity`; the server adopts it in `Terrain263ChunkSource::surviving_block_entities`.

## Configuration

None at runtime. Build range comes from the settings (`Terrain263::min_y`, `height`); the shaped-chunk cache holds 384 chunks (`SHAPED_CAPACITY`). An End gateway with a known exit (the return gateway beside the exit portal) is served as an `end_gateway` block entity carrying it.

## Dependencies

`lodestone-worldgen-core` (`engine::release26_3`), `lodestone-worldgen-data-26-3`, `lodestone-worldgen-feature-26-3`, `lodestone_worldgen::terrain263::parse_state_key` and `lodestone-data` (canonical states and biomes).

Gaps: oracles cover terrain, biomes, carvers and decoration, and the structure oracle covers starts. Generation-time mob packs are Overworld only. The End crystal entities on the pillars are not produced here (decoration builds their bedrock and fire; the dragon fight spawns the crystals).
